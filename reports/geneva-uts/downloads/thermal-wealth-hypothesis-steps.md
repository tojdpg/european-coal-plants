# Geneva UTS - Thermal-Wealth Hypothesis Steps

| Step | What Happened |
|---|---|
| 1. Physical hypothesis | We first mapped candidate cool-value zones from heat, water proximity, canopy, and building fabric. Blue did not mean "expensive"; it meant "physically promising." |
| 2. Visual correction | We switched from orthophoto-only to building-focus maps, because building footprints make the urban pattern much easier to see. |
| 3. Claim correction | We explicitly framed the map as a Thermal-Wealth Hypothesis, not a measured wealth gradient or real-estate result. |
| 4. Price correction | We then added OCSTAT 2024 apartment price per m2 by Geneva sector as the first real-estate proxy. |
| 5. First test | Now we can compare: do high-price sectors overlap more with cool-value candidate buildings than lower-price sectors? |
| 6. Early conclusion | In this first aggregate test, expensive sectors do not simply align with cooler proxy areas. Price seems more tied to central and lakefront urban value, while the cool-value signal appears elsewhere. |

Key sentence for the slide:

**Early result: the cooling premium is not automatic. In Geneva, expensive areas may be valuable despite heat burden, not because they are cooler.**
